## How to read the drafts

- Each page is laid out as **Section → Column → `[Widget: …]`**, so it can be rebuilt in Elementor section by section.
- No colors, fonts or CSS are specified. Everything is meant to use the kit's global colors and typography.
- `[CONFIRM: …]` marks a fact that Bret or SHS must supply or approve before the page goes live. **Nothing marked `[CONFIRM]` has been verified.**
- The quote form on every page is the existing GHL "Master-Lead-Gen" embed (the HTML widget used on the Contact page), not an Elementor Form widget, so leads keep going to the same place.
- Phone numbers are written as `[PHONE]`. On normal site pages CallRail swaps the number, so use the same number widget or link as the site header.

## Where the facts came from

Only these claims are used without a `[CONFIRM]`, because they are already published on seniorhomesolutions.com:

- Over 20 years serving Michigan families; family owned; 5.0 Google rating from 240+ reviews; Certified Aging in Place Specialist on staff; inquiries answered within 24 hours.
- Straight stair lift installs typically take two to four hours (blog: "Straight vs. Curved Stairlifts").
- Curved lifts are custom-built and take two to four weeks from measurement to delivery (same blog post).
- Milford, MI case study: measured on a Sunday, stair lift installed by the end of that week, about two hours on site. The case study names the product "Bruno Elon Stairlift", which looks like a typo for Bruno's Elan model.
- Bruno and Harmar are the straight-lift brands the blog recommends.
- Most stair lifts need 28–30 inches of stair width (blog).
- Two straight lifts "can save $3,000–$7,000 compared to a curved stairlift" (blog). The drafts use this figure instead of the brief's "up to 50% cheaper".

## Open items that affect all four pages

1. **Starting price.** The brief says "Starting at $X,XXX fully installed". The blog already publishes "$3,000 to $5,500 fully installed" for straight lifts and "$8,000 to $15,000 or more" for curved. The drafts use `[CONFIRM: $X,XXX]` and the blog's ranges marked `[CONFIRM]`.
2. **"Up to 50% cheaper" for two straight lifts.** Using the blog's own ranges, two straight lifts cost $6,000–$11,000 against $8,000–$15,000+ for curved. That only reaches 50% at the extremes, so the drafts use the blog's published "$3,000 to $7,000" saving instead. Tell Claude if you want the 50% line and can support it.
3. **How openly to describe the partner-installer model.** The drafts say SHS "coordinates" the install with "vetted local master technicians", as the brief directs. The current site says "our installers" and "our team".
4. **Existing pages that overlap.** `/products/lifts/` ("Stair Lifts", in the main menu) and the blog post "Straight vs. Curved Stairlifts" already target these terms. See the questions in chat before anything is redirected or re-linked.
