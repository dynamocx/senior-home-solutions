## How to read the drafts

- Each page is laid out as **Section → Column → `[Widget: …]`**, so it can be rebuilt in Elementor section by section.
- No colors, fonts or CSS are specified. Everything is meant to use the kit's global colors and typography.
- `[CONFIRM: …]` marks a fact that Bret or SHS must supply or approve before the page goes live. **Nothing marked `[CONFIRM]` has been verified.**
- The quote form on every page is the GHL "Stairlift-Lead-Gen" embed (`stairlift-form-embed.html`), which has its own GHL workflow. It is not an Elementor Form widget.
- Phone numbers are written as `[PHONE]`. On normal site pages CallRail swaps the number, so use the same number widget or link as the site header.

## Where the facts came from

Only these claims are used without a `[CONFIRM]`, because they are already published on seniorhomesolutions.com:

- Over 20 years serving Michigan families; family owned; 5.0 Google rating from 240+ reviews; Certified Aging in Place Specialist on staff; inquiries answered within 24 hours.
- Straight stair lift installs typically take two to four hours (blog: "Straight vs. Curved Stairlifts").
- Curved lifts are custom-built and take two to four weeks from measurement to delivery (same blog post).
- Milford, MI case study: measured on a Sunday, stair lift installed by the end of that week, about two hours on site. The case study names the product "Bruno Elon Stairlift", which looks like a typo for Bruno's Elan model.
- Bruno and Harmar are the straight-lift brands the blog recommends.
- Most stair lifts need 28–30 inches of stair width (blog).
- Two straight lifts "can save $3,000–$7,000 compared to a curved stairlift" (blog). The drafts use this figure instead of the brief's "up to 50% cheaper".

## Decisions (Bret, 2026-10-12)

1. **Starting price:** leave as `$X,XXX` until validated with the client. The blog's ranges stay marked `[CONFIRM]`.
2. **Two-lift saving:** no numbers to support "up to 50%" yet. The drafts use the blog's published "$3,000 to $7,000". Bret is asking the client.
3. **Installer wording:** keep it vague. Use "our installers" and "our team". No mention of partners or subcontractors. Bret is checking exact wording with the client.
4. **`/products/lifts/`:** stays as a product page. Link to the new pages from it, and from the blog posts, after the new pages are published.
5. **Build method:** V4 through the MCP, with styles set on the page (same approach as the Grand Rapids landing page).
6. **Paying for a lift (cost guide):** answers left blank for Bret to vet with the client.
7. **Other missing facts** (warranty and service terms, rentals and used lifts, curved-lift handling, two lifts in one visit): filled with lorem ipsum marked `[PLACEHOLDER]` until the client supplies them.

8. **Service area (2026-10-13):** every sales page and guide gets a "Fast Installation Across Michigan's Major Metros" block near the bottom, linking to `/locations/` and seven metro location pages. Michigan first, with no wording that limits SHS to Michigan forever. The brief's "vetted network of master technicians" line is replaced by "our team", per decision 3.
9. **Static vs dynamic:** pages are static for now (see CLAUDE.md, Stair lift project).
