## Section 4: Why straight costs so much less than curved (2 columns)

**Column 1**
- `[Widget: Heading - H2]` Why a Straight Lift Costs Far Less Than a Curved One
- `[Widget: Text Editor]` The chair and motor are much the same on both. The difference is the rail.

  A straight rail comes in standard lengths and is cut to fit your stairs on the day. A curved rail has to be measured, drawn and fabricated for one staircase only. That custom work is why a curved lift can cost around three times as much and take weeks to arrive.

**Column 2**
- `[Widget: Icon List]`
  - Straight rail: standard, cut to fit, installed in hours
  - Curved rail: custom-built, 2–4 weeks, much higher cost
  - Our advice: use a straight lift whenever the staircase allows it

## Section 5: The two-lift comparison

- `[Widget: Heading - H2]` Two Straight Stair Lifts vs. One Curved Lift
- `[Widget: Text Editor]` If your stairs have a flat landing between two straight flights, you have a choice. One custom curved lift can ride around the turn. Or two straight lifts can cover each flight, with a short walk or transfer across the landing.

  For families where the rider can manage a few steps across the landing, two straight lifts can save $3,000 to $7,000 compared with a curved lift, and can be installed in days instead of weeks.
